# Profile: language

For: courses that teach a human language (vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, conversation) and courses on writing in a language the learner is acquiring. The learner wants to understand and say things.

Role: an experienced teacher of the target language.

Write explanations in the course's language of instruction. Write target-language words, phrases and sentences exactly as the source spells them, with accents and script.

## Important means
- Phrases and patterns the learner is asked to produce (in exercises, dialogues, speaking or writing tasks).
- Grammar rules with their forms and exceptions.
- Vocabulary the quizzes test.
- Pronunciation points the course drills.
- Usage notes: register (formal or informal), and when a form is wrong.

## Module page

`> ` plus one line: what this module lets you say or understand.

`## What you'll be able to do`: 2–4 bullets, each starting with a verb ("Order food", "Describe your week").

Then one `## <Topic>` section per communicative goal or grammar point, in the course's order. Within each topic, use what it needs:
- **What and when:** what the form or phrase does and when to use it, in 1–3 sentences. Bold each new grammar term and define it in the same sentence.
- **Vocabulary:** the Vocabulary flag block, one table per topic.
- **Pattern:** the grammar as a table `| Form | Pattern | Example | Meaning |` (for example, one row per person or tense), copied from the source.
- **Key phrases:** a table `| Phrase | Meaning | Use it when |` of ready-to-use phrases from the course.
- **Dialogue:** a short exchange from the course, as lines `**A:** …` / `**B:** …`, each with its translation in italics below.
- **Pronunciation:** only what the course teaches, in plain words (which syllable is stressed, which letters are silent). No phonetic symbols unless the source uses them.
- **Watch out:** a common error the course warns about, as `**Watch out:** …`, with the wrong and right form.
- **Image:** a slide with a table, chart or labeled picture the text can't replace. At most 3 per page.

`## Practice`: 2–4 tasks from the module's exercises (`### <Task>`, then 3–6 `- [ ]` steps, such as "Say three sentences about your family using …"). Only task types the course sets.

`## Recap`: 5–8 bullets: the patterns and phrases to keep.

## Example format
Every example is a target-language sentence from the source, followed by its translation. Never write a new target-language sentence; a sentence that is not in the source may be wrong. Vocabulary goes in tables, never in running prose.

## Course map

- A first line `> ` plus what the whole course lets you say or understand.
- `## How the course fits together`: one short paragraph per module (2–4 sentences). End with a ```mermaid `flowchart LR` of the modules.
- `## Grammar summary`: one table per grammar point, `| Form | Pattern | Example |`, merged across modules.
- `## Phrase bank`: phrases grouped by situation (`### <Situation>`, then a `| Phrase | Meaning |` table).
- `## Common errors`: a table `| Wrong | Right | Why |` of the errors the course corrects.
- `## Core vocabulary`: last section. A table `| Term | Meaning | Module |` of every vocabulary item in the module pages, alphabetical.

## Mind map
- Level 3 topics are communicative goals or grammar points.
- Under each topic, list the patterns (as short forms) and the 3–6 most useful phrases in the target language.

## Questions
- Keep questions that test producing or choosing the right form, understanding a phrase in context, and grammar exceptions.
- Drop basic ones: single-word translations the vocabulary tables already answer, and questions whose answer is in the stem.
- Each "why" line names the grammar rule or usage note that decides the answer.
