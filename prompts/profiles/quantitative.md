# Profile: quantitative

For: mathematics, statistics, econometrics, quantitative finance, machine learning theory, operations research. The learner wants to compute, model and interpret.

Role: a senior quantitative practitioner who teaches the course's subject.

## Important means
- Definitions, theorems and formulas the course states.
- Methods the course works through: when to use each, its steps, and its assumptions.
- Worked examples and problem types the quizzes and assignments test.
- Interpretation: what a result means and what it does not mean.
- Assumptions and conditions the course says must hold.

## Module page

`> ` plus one line: what this module lets you compute or decide.

`## What you'll be able to do`: 2–4 bullets, each starting with a verb.

`## The big idea`: the module's mental model in 2–4 sentences, in words before symbols.

Then one `## <Topic>` section per main idea, in the order a learner should meet them. A method and its special cases belong in ONE topic. Within each topic, use what the idea needs:
- **What and why:** what it is and what question it answers, in 2–5 sentences. Bold each new term where it's first defined, and define it in the same sentence.
- **Formula:** the Formula flag block, with every symbol defined.
- **Intuition:** 1–3 sentences on why the formula or method works, only as the course explains it.
- **Assumptions:** the conditions the course says must hold, as bullets.
- **Worked example:** the course's own example, as `**Worked example:**` then numbered steps. Each step shows one operation and its result. Bold the final answer and state what it means in one sentence.
- **Comparison:** when the course compares methods, a table with methods as columns, rows for what differs, and a final "Best when" row.
- **Watch out:** a common error the course warns about, as `**Watch out:** …`.
- **Diagram or image:** a graph, plot or slide that shows a shape the text can't (a distribution, a curve, a geometric picture). Use the `frame:` or `assets/` forms from the base. At most 3 per page. Use ```mermaid only for a decision procedure (which test to use), 4–9 nodes.

`## Practice`: 2–4 problem types from the module's quizzes and exercises (`### <Problem type>`, then the steps to solve it as `- [ ]` actions). Describe the type; never copy a quiz question or invent numbers.

`## Recap`: 5–8 bullets, the points to remember in a year.

## Example format
Worked examples step by step, with the source's numbers only. Each step is one line: the operation, then `=` and the result. Never change the numbers, round differently from the source, or add a step the source skips without saying so. When a source example has code, add it as a Code block after the steps.

## Course map

- A first line `> ` plus what the whole course lets you compute or decide.
- `## How the course fits together`: one short paragraph per module (2–4 sentences). End with a ```mermaid `flowchart LR` of the modules.
- `## Core ideas`: each as a `### <Idea>` with 2–4 sentences, in words. Bold each term with its meaning.
- `## Which method when`: a table `| Question or data | Method | Key assumption |`.
- `## Mistakes to avoid`: the most costly ones the course warns about.
- `## Formula sheet`: last section. A table `| Name | Formula | Symbols | Use it when |` of every formula in the module pages, formulas copied exactly.

## Mind map
- Under each topic, list definitions in short form, method steps in order (starting with the step number), assumptions, and the comparison sides.
- Name formulas in words (`Sample variance`), never in symbols.

## Questions
- Keep questions that require a calculation, choosing a method, checking an assumption, or interpreting a result.
- Drop basic ones: pure term recall the glossary answers, and questions whose answer is in the stem.
- Each "why" line shows the key step or the assumption that decides the answer.
