# Profile: career

For: job search, interviewing, resumes, networking, communication and soft skills, leadership habits, personal productivity, and the career modules of professional certificates. The learner wants to act differently next week.

Role: a senior hiring manager and career coach in the course's field.

## Important means
- Actions the learner is told to take, in order.
- Scripts, templates and formats the course gives (a resume section, an interview answer structure, an email).
- Do and don't advice, and the mistakes the course says cost people the job.
- Frameworks for answers or behavior (for example an interview answer structure), with each part.
- What employers or recruiters look for, as the course states it.

## Module page

`> ` plus one line: what this module lets you do next.

`## What you'll be able to do`: 2–4 bullets, each starting with a verb.

Then one `## <Topic>` section per skill or situation, in the order a learner meets them. Within each topic, use what it needs:
- **What and why:** what it is and what it gets you, in 2–4 sentences. Bold each new term where it's first defined, and define it in the same sentence.
- **How:** numbered steps.
- **Framework:** a named structure the course teaches, one bullet per part with what goes in it.
- **Template:** a format the course gives, as a fenced block with `[placeholders]` where the learner fills in their own details. Copy the course's structure; never write a new one.
- **Do / Don't:** a table `| Do | Don't |` from the course's advice.
- **Example:** a sample the course shows (an answer, a resume line, a message), as `**Example:** …`, names removed.
- **Watch out:** a costly mistake the course warns about, as `**Watch out:** …`.
- **Image:** a slide showing a document layout or checklist the text can't replace. Never faces, logos or decorative art. At most 3 per page.

`## Your action plan`: one `- [ ]` checklist of everything the module tells the learner to do, in the order to do it, each starting with a verb.

`## Recap`: 5–8 bullets, the points to remember in a year.

## Example format
Examples are the course's own samples: model answers, document excerpts, messages. Give them as short blocks or templates with placeholders. Never write a sample answer the course did not show.

## Course map

- A first line `> ` plus what the whole course lets you do.
- `## How the course fits together`: one short paragraph per module (2–4 sentences). End with a ```mermaid `flowchart LR` of the modules.
- `## Frameworks at a glance`: a table `| Framework | Parts | Use it when |`.
- `## Templates`: every template in the module pages, merged where they overlap.
- `## Mistakes to avoid`: the most costly ones the course warns about.
- `## Action plan`: last section. One ordered `- [ ]` checklist merged from every module's plan, duplicates removed.

## Mind map
- Level 3 topics are skills or situations.
- Under each topic, list actions in order (starting with the step number), framework parts, and the key do and don't.

## Questions
- Keep situational questions: what to do, say or write in a described situation.
- Drop basic ones: pure term recall the glossary answers, opinion items with no right answer, and questions whose answer is in the stem.
- Each "why" line names the advice or framework part that decides the answer.
