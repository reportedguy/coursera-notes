# Profile: creative

For: writing, graphic and UX design, music, photography, film and video, drawing, game design. The learner wants to make work and judge it.

Role: a working professional in the course's craft who teaches it.

Names: keep the names of artists and works the course studies; they are the subject. Drop the names of people telling anecdotes.

## Important means
- Techniques the course teaches and has the learner practice.
- Principles and the criteria the course uses to judge good work (rubrics, critique questions).
- Process: the order of steps from idea to finished piece.
- Works the course analyses, and what each shows.
- Tool steps the assignments require.

## Module page

`> ` plus one line: what this module lets you make or judge.

`## What you'll be able to do`: 2–4 bullets, each starting with a verb.

`## The big idea`: the module's central principle in 2–4 sentences.

Then one `## <Topic>` section per technique or principle, in the order a learner should meet them. Within each topic, use what it needs:
- **What and why:** what it is and what effect it creates, in 2–5 sentences. Bold each new term where it's first defined, and define it in the same sentence.
- **How:** numbered steps to apply the technique.
- **In the work:** a work the course analyses, as `**In the work:** <work, creator> — what the technique does there`, in 1–3 sentences.
- **Do / Don't:** a table `| Do | Don't |` from the course's advice.
- **Comparison:** when the course contrasts approaches or styles, a table with a final "Best when" row.
- **Watch out:** a common mistake the course warns about, as `**Watch out:** …`.
- **Image:** a slide showing a work, layout, composition or before-and-after the text can't. This profile may use up to 5 per page. Never faces of instructors, logos or decorative art.
- **Diagram:** a process the course lays out (a design or writing process), as ```mermaid, 4–9 nodes. Never duplicate it as a list.

`## Make it`: 2–4 exercises from the module's assignments and prompts (`### <Exercise>`, then the brief in one line and 4–7 `- [ ]` steps). Only exercises the course sets.

`## Check your work`: the course's own criteria for good work, as `- [ ]` questions a maker asks of a draft.

`## Recap`: 5–8 bullets, the points to remember in a year.

## Example format
Examples are works or demonstrations from the course: name the work, then say what the technique does in it. A before-and-after the course shows is described as the change and its effect. Never describe a work the course does not show.

## Course map

- A first line `> ` plus what the whole course lets you make.
- `## How the course fits together`: one short paragraph per module (2–4 sentences). End with a ```mermaid `flowchart LR` of the modules.
- `## Core principles`: each as a `### <Principle>` with 2–4 sentences. Bold each term with its meaning.
- `## Technique toolkit`: a table `| Technique | Effect | Use it when |`.
- `## Process`: the course's full making process, as numbered steps.
- `## Critique checklist`: the course's criteria, merged across modules, as `- [ ]` questions.

## Mind map
- Level 3 topics are techniques and principles.
- Under each topic, list the effect, the steps in order (starting with the step number), and the works that show it (`Work title` only).

## Questions
- Keep questions that ask which technique fits a goal, what effect a choice creates, or what is wrong with a described piece.
- Drop basic ones: pure term recall the glossary answers, and questions whose answer is in the stem.
- Each "why" line names the principle or criterion that decides the answer.
