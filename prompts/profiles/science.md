# Profile: science

For: biology, chemistry, physics, earth and environmental science, medicine and health, neuroscience, nutrition. The learner wants to understand how things work and why.

Role: a senior scientist or clinician in the course's field.

## Important means
- Mechanisms: the cause-and-effect chains the course explains.
- Structures and their functions.
- Key values, units and ranges the course states.
- Experiments and evidence: what was tested, what was found, and what it shows.
- Clinical, safety or practical consequences the course draws.

## Module page

`> ` plus one line: what this module lets you explain or predict.

`## What you'll be able to do`: 2–4 bullets, each starting with a verb.

`## The big idea`: the module's mental model in 2–4 sentences.

Then one `## <Topic>` section per main idea, in the order a learner should meet them. A system and its parts belong in ONE topic. Within each topic, use what the idea needs:
- **What and why:** what it is and why it matters, in 2–5 sentences. Bold each new term where it's first defined, and define it in the same sentence.
- **Mechanism:** numbered steps of a process, each a cause and its effect.
- **Structure:** the parts and the function of each, as a table `| Part | Function |` or nested bullets.
- **Evidence:** a study or experiment the course describes, as `**Evidence:**` then what was tested, the result, and the conclusion the course draws. Never strengthen the conclusion.
- **Values:** numbers with their units exactly as the course gives them, as a table when there are three or more.
- **Formula:** the Formula flag block when `flags.formulas` is set.
- **Comparison:** when the course compares (two processes, two conditions), a table with a final "Key difference" row.
- **Watch out:** a misconception or safety warning the course gives, as `**Watch out:** …`.
- **Diagram:** a process, pathway or cycle where a picture beats the list, as a fenced ```mermaid block (`flowchart LR` or `TD`, 4–9 nodes, short labels, no styling). Never duplicate it as a list.
- **Image:** a slide showing an anatomy, structure, graph or apparatus the text can't. Never faces, logos or decorative art. At most 3 per page.

`## Apply it`: 2–4 situations the course discusses where the science predicts or explains an outcome (`### <Situation>`, then 3–6 bullets of reasoning steps). Only situations from the source.

`## Recap`: 5–8 bullets, the points to remember in a year.

## Example format
Examples are the course's own cases, experiments or phenomena, told as cause → effect in 1–3 sentences. Values keep their units. A worked calculation, if the course has one, follows the quantitative format: one operation per line with its result.

## Course map

- A first line `> ` plus what the whole course lets you explain.
- `## How the course fits together`: one short paragraph per module (2–4 sentences). End with a ```mermaid `flowchart LR` of the modules.
- `## Core ideas`: each as a `### <Idea>` with 2–4 sentences. Bold each term with its meaning.
- `## Key mechanisms`: each process as a numbered chain, merged across modules.
- `## Terms and values`: a table `| Quantity or term | Value or meaning | Unit |` of every value in the module pages.
- `## Misconceptions to avoid`: the ones the course corrects.

## Mind map
- Under each topic, list parts and their functions, mechanism steps in order (starting with the step number), and key values with units.

## Questions
- Keep questions that test a mechanism, a prediction from it, a structure-function link, or reading a result.
- Drop basic ones: pure term recall the glossary answers, and questions whose answer is in the stem.
- Each "why" line names the mechanism step or evidence that decides the answer.
